Daily Math: Powers of 10 and Exponents

Ten questions. Powers of 10 are a secret shortcut: just count the zeros! 💪

📌 Part A: What is an exponent?

An exponent tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself.

10³ = 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000

The small raised number (the exponent) tells you how many zeros follow the 1 when the base is 10. So 10² = 100, 10⁴ = 10,000, and 10¹ = 10.

💡 Need a hint?

📌 Part B: Multiplying and dividing by powers of 10

When you multiply by 10, 100, or 1,000, every digit moves to a bigger place value (the decimal point moves right). When you divide, the digits move to a smaller place value (the decimal point moves left).

3.4 × 10² = 3.4 × 100 = 340
45 ÷ 10 = 4.5

💡 Need a hint?

Move the decimal point 1 place to the right.

💡 Need a hint?

10² = 100. Move the decimal point 2 places to the right.

💡 Need a hint?

10³ = 1,000. Move the decimal point 3 places to the right: 8 → 8,000.

💡 Need a hint?

Divide by 100. Move the decimal point 2 places to the left.
