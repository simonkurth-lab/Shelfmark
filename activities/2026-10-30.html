Lena had practiced her speech eleven times, but when Ms. Ortiz called her name, her hands went cold. Twenty-six faces turned toward her. She walked to the front of the room and unfolded her notecard, which was shaking so much the words looked blurry.

"My topic is the old library on Fourth Street," she began. Her voice came out small. Someone in the back row coughed. Lena wanted to sit down and pretend she was sick.

Then she remembered why she had chosen this topic. The city planned to close the library in the spring, and her little brother went there every Saturday. If nobody spoke up, it would simply disappear.

Lena took a breath and looked up from her card. "That library is where my brother learned to read," she said, louder now. "And I think it's worth saving."

By the end, her hands were still shaking. But three classmates came up afterward to ask how they could help, and Lena realized that being brave did not mean feeling calm. It meant speaking anyway.
